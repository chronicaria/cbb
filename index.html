How the model works

  • Ratings. Published in points against an average D1 team: 0 is average, and on a neutral court a +10 team is favoured by 10 points over an average one. A game's spread is the difference of the two ratings plus home court and the other context terms. The engine runs in Elo underneath; one point is — Elo.
  • Priors. With 360-odd teams there are no Vegas season lines to start from, so each team opens from last season's final rating pulled toward its conference's level, preseason efficiency margins from KenPom and Bart Torvik, or a blend; a team new to D1 starts from a fitted level. Best in testing: —.
  • Updates. After every game between two D1 teams both sides move by K × a margin-of-victory multiplier × (result − expected). Margins are capped, so blowouts stop counting past the cap, and an autocorrelation term shrinks the multiplier when a favourite wins big. Games against non-D1 opponents count in the record but move no ratings.
  • Game context. Home court shifts a game's expected result (not at neutral sites). Each other factor was tested by leave-one-season-out fits and stays in only if it lowers held-out log loss. In: —. Out: —.
  • Forecast. The rest of the schedule is played — times with ratings updating game by game, then every conference tournament from its own bracket, the NCAA selection (automatic bids, then at-large teams by rating and strength of record), S-curve seeding with the Opening Round (the 76-team field from 2027), and the bracket.
